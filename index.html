What's on the radar

  • Nobody has added up how much of the AI profit boom is companies revaluing each other

    Four company-quarters are documented, and the mechanism is clear (see the article below). What's missing is the total: across every large company holding a stake in a private AI lab, what share of reported profit growth comes from those marks rather than from selling things. No research house appears to have published that figure. Third-quarter results, out from mid-October, are the next chance to find out whether this is spreading or staying with a handful of names.

  • Private lenders may be exposed to AI twice, in opposite directions

    Investment funds that lend privately have poured hundreds of billions into financing AI data centres. Separately, software companies are about 20% of their loan books — and software is one of the industries most likely to be disrupted by AI. These look like two unrelated bets. If AI capital spending disappoints, both could sour at once: the data-centre loans because the revenue never arrived, the software loans because their borrowers were undermined anyway. Meanwhile private-credit defaults are at their highest in at least five years. Whether any fund actually tracks these two exposures as connected is unknown.

  • Why the profit in selling same-day options hasn't been competed away

    Either sellers are being paid for a rare disaster that genuinely can't be insured cheaply, or it just needs tools ordinary investors don't have. A second source now leans the same way as the first, and says so outright: the premium on individual stocks runs many times larger than on the index — Tesla's roughly 11× the S&P's — because company-specific risk is expensive to hedge. Suggestive, but still nobody has run the clean test: does the profit shrink as the funds packaging this trade grow?

  • The dissenting Fed officials turned out to have the boring reason

    Three officials voted to raise rates rather than hold. The interesting theory was that they were really arguing interest rates can't fix supply-driven inflation at all. Reading all three of their own statements settles it: none of them makes that argument. All three point to current conditions — inflation that won't come down, demand pressure, a labour market that isn't being restrained. A plain explanation beat a clever one, which is the usual outcome and worth recording as such.

When an AI startup raises money, its investors book a profit

16 August 2026

Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft have all reported billions in profit from revaluing stakes they hold in private AI labs. An accounting rule ties those gains to the labs' fundraising calendar rather than to any business performance — and remove two companies and the S&P 500's record earnings beat shrinks by two-thirds.

The AI trade sits in most portfolios twice

6 August 2026

Lending to AI companies by private investment funds went from near zero to over $200bn, with $800bn more expected. If you hold an index fund and some private credit, you may own the same bet twice — and nothing on your statement shows it.

Ten companies are now 40% of the S&P 500, and that worries people. A study covering 1926 to 2025 tested the obvious response and found it earned less, swung more, and ended with less than half the money.

Small investors crowded into the one options trade that reliably loses

5 August 2026

Same-day contracts are over 75% of small investors' S&P 500 options activity and lose 4.7% relative to everything else. The cause is a hidden fee rather than bad predictions — and the real profit goes to whoever's on the other side.
